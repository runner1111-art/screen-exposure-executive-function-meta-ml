# Machine-learning figure legends

**Figure ML1 | Comparative predictive performance under nested cohort-grouped validation.** A, Group-balanced root mean squared error (RMSE) across five repetitions of five-fold outer validation. B, Predictive Q², where zero denotes performance equivalent to predicting the validation-set mean and negative values indicate poorer prediction. Hyperparameters and all preprocessing operations were estimated only within the corresponding outer training data using four-fold grouped inner validation. Points denote repeated partitions; boxes show their empirical distributions. C, Observed versus averaged out-of-fold predictions from the best-performing non-null model, with colours denoting executive-function domains. The diagonal marks perfect prediction. D, Out-of-fold residuals against predicted values; the red line is a descriptive linear smooth. Effects from the same cohort or sample cluster were kept together in all splits, and performance metrics assigned equal total weight to each independent cluster.

**Figure ML2 | Exploratory sources of predictive variation and error.** A, Change in held-out RMSE after group-block permutation of each predictor in the selected model. Squares show mean changes and horizontal lines show empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentiles across outer folds and permutations; values spanning zero indicate unstable predictive contribution. B,C, Partial-dependence functions for baseline mean age and log sample size from the full-data refit. Rug marks indicate observed support. These curves describe model dependence and are not causal estimates. D, Group-balanced mean absolute error (MAE) by executive-function domain. Squares show equal-cluster mean errors and horizontal lines show cluster-bootstrap 95% intervals; *g* is the number of contributing independent cohort/sample clusters.

**Figure ML3 | Proxy-augmented sensitivity analysis and heterogeneity in prediction error.** A,B, RMSE and predictive Q² for the training-mean comparator and selected ridge model in the expanded convertible-effect dataset versus the frozen proxy-augmented sensitivity dataset. Squares show means across five repeated outer partitions and lines show empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentiles. C, Numbers of convertible effects and explicitly flagged proxy statistics by executive-function domain. D, Group-balanced MAE by screen-exposure category; *g* denotes the number of independent cohort/sample clusters. Proxy statistics were retained as a separately labelled sensitivity analysis because they are not guaranteed to be commensurate with Fisher-transformed correlations.

**Figure ML4 | Cross-cohort learning curves for prediction of screen exposure–executive function associations.** A, Held-out root mean squared error (RMSE) for the selected ridge model and a training-mean comparator as the number of independent training cohort/sample clusters increased from 8 to 64. B, Paired difference in RMSE between ridge and the comparator; values below zero favour ridge. C, Held-out predictive Q² for both approaches; zero denotes performance equivalent to the validation-set mean. Points are means from 40 repeated group holdouts with five independent clusters reserved for testing, and bands or lines show empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentiles. The ridge penalty was fixed at α = 100, selected in the nested primary analysis. At 64 training clusters, the mean RMSE difference was −0.0067 (empirical 95% interval −0.0385 to 0.0325) and mean predictive Q² remained negative, indicating sensitivity to which cohorts were held out.

**Figure ML5 | Cohort-level heterogeneity in out-of-fold prediction error and calibration bias.** A, Mean absolute error (MAE) for each independent cohort/sample cluster from repeated nested grouped-validation predictions. B, Mean prediction bias, calculated as predicted minus observed Fisher's z; positive values indicate predictions that were less negative or more positive than observed. Squares show repeat-averaged values and horizontal lines show empirical 2.5th–97.5th percentiles across the repeats in which the cluster was held out. Cohorts are ordered by MAE. Large errors concentrated in a small number of clusters identify transportability challenges and should not be interpreted as evidence that those individual studies are invalid.

**Figure ML6 | Prediction error and evidence support across executive-function and screen-exposure categories.** A, Group-balanced MAE for each executive-function domain by screen-exposure category combination. Cells based on fewer than three independent clusters are labelled sparse and are not assigned a stable error estimate. B, Corresponding evidence support, where *g* is the number of independent cohort/sample clusters and *k* is the number of effect sizes. The largest supported errors occurred for global executive function with interactive/gaming exposure, inhibitory control with interactive/gaming exposure, and inhibitory control with social-media or multitasking exposure. These patterns locate areas of poor model transportability; they do not estimate subgroup causal effects.

**Figure ML7 | Stability of algorithm and hyperparameter selection under grouped validation.** A, Number of outer folds in which each algorithm achieved the lowest inner-validation RMSE. B, Outer-fold RMSE difference from the training-mean comparator; values below zero favour the model. Points show the 25 outer folds (five repetitions of five-fold validation), and box plots summarise their distributions. C, Frequency with which each ridge penalty was selected. Ridge won 14 of 25 inner-validation contests, followed by elastic net and random forest (four each) and gradient boosting (three). Ridge also produced the lowest mean repeated outer-validation RMSE, and α = 100 was selected in all 25 folds. The heavy shrinkage and modest RMSE gain indicate weak, distributed multivariable signal rather than a clinically deployable prediction model.

**Figure ML8 | Magnitude and directional stability of predictors in the final regularised linear model.** A, Mean absolute contribution of each predictor block to fitted Fisher's z in the full-data ridge refit. B, Distribution of cluster-mean signed contributions, with orange lines marking medians and the dashed line denoting zero. Categorical dummy variables were recombined into their parent predictor blocks before contributions were summarised. The panels are descriptive diagnostics of the final refit and are not held-out importance estimates, causal effects or evidence of statistical significance; inferential emphasis should remain on the grouped-validation performance and permutation analysis in Figures ML1 and ML2.
